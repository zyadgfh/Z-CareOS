# 04 --- Phase 1: Market & Customer Discovery

**Indicative duration:** 3--4 weeks\
**Primary outcome:** Evidence that a specific solo-practitioner segment
has a frequent, costly problem and a credible willingness to pay.

## 1. Research questions

1.  Which specialty has the most painful, frequent, and underserved
    workflow?
2.  Which tasks consume the practitioner's time every day?
3.  What systems, spreadsheets, paper processes, and messaging tools are
    used now?
4.  What causes errors, missed appointments, duplicated work, or lost
    revenue?
5.  What must be done on a phone, and what is better on a larger screen?
6.  What makes a practitioner switch from an existing system?
7.  What data migration, privacy, reliability, and support expectations
    are mandatory?
8.  What price and billing structure are acceptable?

## 2. Research sample

-   20--30 interviews with solo practitioners.
-   Include multiple candidate specialties; do not over-sample one
    specialty before evidence.
-   Test 6--8 competitors hands-on where access is available.
-   Seek 3--5 potential pilot clinics only after the problem and
    proposed value are sufficiently clear.

Candidate specialties for investigation---not a preselected decision: -
Dentistry. - Dermatology. - Physiotherapy. - General practice.

## 4. Competitor test protocol

For each competitor, assess: - signup and onboarding friction; - mobile
usability and actual task completion; - patient record workflow; -
appointment and queue workflow; - specialty-specific clinical
documentation; - prescription and billing flow; - import/export and data
portability; - permissions and auditability; - pricing, setup fees,
usage limits, and overages; - support response and documentation; -
cancellation and data retrieval process.

Use a 0--5 score only when there is evidence. Mark unknown as **Not
Tested**, not zero.

## 5. Interview protocol

Ask about past behavior, not hypothetical enthusiasm: - "Walk me through
your last working day." - "Show me how you handled the last patient from
booking to follow-up." - "Where did you repeat data entry?" - "What
happened the last time a record, appointment, or payment was wrong?" -
"What do you currently pay for software, staff time, or workarounds?" -
"What have you tried and stopped using? Why?" - "What would make
switching too risky?" - "Can we observe a typical workflow using dummy
or safely anonymized material?"

Do not collect identifiable patient information. Do not request patient
records or screenshots containing personal health data.

## 6. Evidence capture

For each interview, record: - participant code, specialty, practice
type, and relevant context; - workflow observed; - pain point and
frequency; - consequence and current workaround; - existing tools and
spend, if voluntarily disclosed; - desired outcome; - objections and
switching barriers; - evidence strength: observed / specific past
example / opinion; - follow-up permission.

Obtain appropriate permission before recording. Store research notes
securely and minimize personal data.

## 7. Pricing validation

Test price concepts at **EGP 399 / 599 / 799 per month** as research
probes, not approved prices. Clarify what is included, billing cadence,
setup fees, message usage, support, and data export. Ask for behavioral
evidence such as willingness to join a pilot or pay a
refundable/approved deposit only if separately authorized and legally
appropriate. Do not collect payment as part of this planning task.

## 8. Specialty selection scorecard

Score each specialty from 1--5 using evidence: - pain severity and
frequency --- 25%; - willingness to pay --- 20%; - underserved workflow
gap --- 20%; - mobile suitability --- 15%; - reachable pilot access ---
10%; - delivery and compliance feasibility --- 10%.

A weighted score is a decision aid, not a substitute for judgment.
Document confidence and sample bias.

## 9. Deliverables

-   Competitor teardown report.
-   Interview synthesis and anonymized evidence.
-   Workflow maps for leading specialties.
-   Specialty comparison scorecard.
-   Pricing research summary.
-   Opportunity brief with strongest problem statement.
-   Recommendation: Go / Pivot / Stop / More Research.

## 10. Exit gate G1

Pass only if: - a repeated, important problem is supported by specific
evidence; - the target segment is sufficiently defined; - the initial
specialty recommendation is evidence-based; - there is credible
willingness to pay; - 3--5 plausible pilot participants can be
approached; - the differentiation is stronger than generic feature
parity.

If these conditions fail, extend research, pivot the segment/specialty,
or stop. Do not begin the full MVP build.
