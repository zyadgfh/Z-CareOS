# 09 --- Phase 6: Hybrid Pilot

**Indicative duration:** 4--6 weeks\
**Initial cohort target:** 3--5 solo-practitioner clinics\
**Policy:** Hybrid pilot with clear trial limits and an explicit,
consent-based paid transition.

## 1. Pilot objectives

-   Verify repeated use in real practice.
-   Measure workflow value and friction.
-   Validate onboarding and support load.
-   Test willingness to pay.
-   Identify product gaps that block retention.
-   Validate operational and security controls under real usage.

## 2. Entry conditions

Before any real patient data is processed: - qualified legal review of
applicable requirements is complete; - privacy notices, contracts,
permissions, and responsibilities are approved; - security review and
penetration testing are complete to the agreed scope; - tenant
isolation, access controls, audit, encryption, backups, and restore have
passed; - incident response and support escalation are ready; - pilot
clinics understand scope, limitations, data handling, support, fees, and
exit/export process; - no critical security or legal blocker remains.

If these conditions are not met, run a simulated pilot using synthetic
data only.

## 3. Pilot onboarding

For each clinic: 1. Confirm eligibility and pilot agreement. 2. Explain
scope, known limitations, data responsibilities, and support channel. 3.
Configure account and permissions. 4. Train the practitioner on the
primary workflow. 5. Verify export and account recovery path. 6. Start
with a limited cohort/workflow if appropriate. 7. Conduct scheduled
check-ins. 8. Capture issues without collecting unnecessary patient
data.

## 4. Hybrid commercial policy

-   A free or discounted trial may be offered for a defined period and
    scope.
-   Conversion to paid use must be explicit and transparent.
-   Do not silently charge or silently change plan terms.
-   Do not use subscription status to create unsafe access to clinical
    records.
-   Define data export and account closure before pilot enrollment.
-   Define support boundaries and response expectations.

## 5. Pilot measurement

Collect: - activation and onboarding completion; - weekly active clinics
and practitioners; - completion of core workflow; - task time and error
rate; - repeat usage and retention; - support tickets per clinic; -
user-reported value; - willingness to pay and objections; - conversion
to paid plan; - reliability and security events.

Do not put identifiable patient data into product analytics.

## 6. Pilot review cadence

-   Daily monitoring of critical errors and service health.
-   Weekly clinic check-ins.
-   Weekly product/engineering review of blockers.
-   Midpoint review for continuation or intervention.
-   Final structured interview and outcome review.

## 7. Exit gate G6

Suggested initial thresholds (to be recalibrated against the selected
specialty and cohort): - 3--5 clinics complete onboarding. - At least
70% of pilot clinics are active weekly. - At least 70% remain active
after four weeks. - At least 80% of users can complete the primary
workflow without assistance after onboarding. - Three clinics show
credible intent to continue on a paid plan; actual conversion is
stronger evidence. - No unresolved critical security/privacy incident. -
Support burden and reliability are operationally sustainable.

These are hypotheses for the pilot, not proven industry benchmarks. If
the sample is too small, report uncertainty rather than claiming
statistical proof.

## 8. Possible outcomes

-   **Go:** strong repeated use, value, safety, and willingness to pay.
-   **Iterate:** clear value but fixable onboarding or workflow
    friction.
-   **Pivot:** pain exists but specialty, workflow, platform, or price
    is wrong.
-   **Stop:** no compelling problem or viable economics, or risks cannot
    be acceptably mitigated.
